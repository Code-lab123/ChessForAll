King's Indian Attack

The King's Indian Attack is one of the most reliable and solid openings for White, starting with 1.Nf3. Instead of playing in tactical pawn battles, White sets up a solid kingside fianchetto structure that can be played against almost any response from Black. This makes it an incredibly safe opening where White avoids early traps, secures a good position, and prepares to launch a powerful attack later in the game.

The Core Move Sequence


  1. Nf3 d5 (White develops the knight flexibly; Black occupies the center)
  2. g3 Nf6 (White prepares to fianchetto the light-squared bishop; Black develops naturally)
  3. Bg2 e6 (White positions the bishop on the long diagonal; Black solidifies their center)
  4. d3 c5 (White controls the e4 square and prepares the central push; Black claims space)
  5. O-O Nc6 (White castles the king; Black brings out the queenside knight)

White's Objectives

The e4-e5 Push: White aims to put a powerful pawn on the e5 square, locking down space and using it as a spacial advantage for a kingside attack.
The Pawn Pyramid: White protects the entire position with an unbreakable kingside structure and flexible pawn chains, rendering Black's early counter-attacks useless.

Black's Objectives

The Queenside Counter: Black tries to open up the queenside by expanding with b5 and c4 to create a route for minor piece manuveres.
Neutralizing the Bishop: Black often brings their own pieces or pawns to contest the long h1-a8 diagonal to challenge White's fianchettoed g2-bishop and break White's position.
