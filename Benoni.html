Benoni

The Benoni Defense is an strong attacking and defensive opening with tactical play from Black. By striking the center with 1...c5, Black wants White to push d4 - d5, creating a pyramid pawn structure that sets up the midgame. This modern defense leads to highly tactical positions where Black intentionally sacrifices space and accepts a backward d6-pawn in exchange for an active queenside majority, open files, and fast counter-play.

The Core Move Sequence


  1. d4 Nf6 (The standard response to the queen's pawn; Black delays central commitments)
  2. c4 c5 (The defining Benoni strike, immediately challenging White's central d-pawn)
  3. d5 e6 (White locks down space by advancing; Black chips away at the wedge)
  4. Nc3 exd5 (White develops a knight; Black trades pawns to open up the central files)
  5. cxd5 d6 (White reclaims material with the c-pawn; Black secures a blockade on the backward d-pawn)

White's Objectives

The Central Attack: White aims to use the massive space advantage by preparing a well-timed e4-e5 breakthrough to quickly attack Black's pieces.
Kingside Breakthrough: White leverages control over the center to kick-start piece maneuvers onto kingside, aiming to break open Black's castled pawn structure.

Black's Objectives

The Queenside Pawn Storm: Black focuses on a fast pawn majority on the queenside using a7-a6 and b7-b5 to open lines for the rooks.
The Bishop: Black relies heavily on the fianchettoed dark-squared bishop on g7, using it like a long-range sniper to pressure White’s entire queenside structure.
