Italian Game

The Italian Game is one of the oldest, most classic, and highly instructional openings in chess, starting with 1.e4. Instead of pinning Black's knight like in the Ruy Lopez, White places the light-squared bishop on the c4 square. This immediately aims a direct attack at Black's weakest point—the vulnerable f7-pawn—setting up highly tactical battles or slow, strategic maneuvers from the opening.

The Core Move Sequence


  1. e4 e5 (The classic open game double king's pawn baseline)
  2. Nf3 Nc6 (White attacks the e5 pawn; Black develops a knight to defend it)
  3. Bc4 Bc5 (The defining move of the Italian Game; Black mirrors White's active bishop placement)
  4. c3 Nf6 (White prepares to build a central pawn duo; Black strikes back at the e4 pawn)
  5. d3 d6 (White builds a modest pawn chain; Black secures the central majority)

White's Objectives

The F7 Weakness: White aims to attack Black's f7-square by using a kingside knight jump to g5 with the c4-bishop.
The Giuoco Piano Squeeze: White uses an solid c3-d3-h3 pawn barrier to quietly build a spatial advantage before using knights to attack Black's Kingside.

Black's Objectives

The D5 Equalizer: Black fights to stop White's central attack by timing an important d6–d5 pawn strike to seize more space.
Minor Piece Attack: Black looks to trade off White's c4-bishop using knight maneuvers to b6 or a5, achieving a perfectly equal endgame.
